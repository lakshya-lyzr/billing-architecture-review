Recommended direction

Autumn reduces more of Pagos’s credit-management work.

Our hardest requirement is concurrent admission to one organization credit pool across several products. Autumn provides a reserve / confirm / release lifecycle that we exercised locally. With Lago Community, we would still need to own this runtime accounting layer.

Conditional adoption

Consolidated recurring billing, scoped credits, independent product plans and a monthly commitment passed locally. The monthly-reset and qualified replacement gaps have additional local fixes; production integration and deployment gates remain. Shared SDK and reporting checks used validation-only prototypes.

Autumn also has the heavier self-hosting recipe in the version tested. Functional simplification does not mean simpler infrastructure.

01 / DECISION CONTEXT

The system we need to support

One payer, several products

An organization can buy Alpha Pro and an Architect plan independently. Product access stays distinct, while eligible included credits and paid top-ups fund one shared spending pool.

Credit grants retain their source, scope, expiry and spending order. Shared credits are the default; a plan or promotion may explicitly restrict a grant to one product. Cancelling one product must not remove another product’s access or unrelated purchased credits.

One shared integration contract

Every product reserves before expensive work, records actual usage, and settles the same operation. Products should not each implement balance arithmetic, token pricing, usage ledgers, retries and Stripe webhooks.

Pagos becomes the common service behind that contract rather than disappearing.

Evidence boundary. Autumn received extensive local Stripe sandbox and concurrency validation. Lago received documentation and source review, not the same end-to-end test suite. “Not established” below is an evaluation limit, not proof that a feature can never exist.
01A / AGREED COMMERCIAL MODEL

Model C: shared credits, product-owned packaging

The selected direction follows the recommendation in Should Lyzr credits be shared? (15 October 2026). Its descriptions of current product pricing include items marked “to confirm”; those are context, not independently verified requirements or test results.

Shared by the platform

  • One credit balance per customer organization; shared use is the default.
  • One payment-method experience, central checkout, tax and refund handling.
  • Consolidated invoicing, with billing periods and exceptions explicitly defined.
  • One customer pricing agreement, central free-credit policy, alerts and top-ups.
  • One credit denomination: 1 credit = $1, using fixed-point amounts.

Owned by each product team

  • Plans, feature packaging, subscription prices and usage rates.
  • Promotions and trials within the shared free-credit policy.
  • In-product checkout entry points, upgrade prompts and messaging.
  • Reliable capture of actual usage and product attribution.
  • Finance approval of commercial rates is the proposed governance rule, not an implemented workflow.
Ownership of prices is different from execution of prices. Product teams define their rates. Pagos stores approved, versioned rates and applies them through the common metering service. Products report usage facts instead of maintaining independent authoritative charge calculations. Provider cost and customer charge remain separate.

03 / WHAT PAGOS STOPS OWNING

Autumn removes the credit engine, not our business context

With Autumn

Delegate: recurring grants, spendable credit balances, deduction order, atomic holds and settlement, child spending limits, plan lifecycle and Stripe billing orchestration.

Pagos keeps: organization and product mapping, shared SDK/API, usage ledger, approved product and enterprise rate versions, grant-scope policy, free-credit eligibility, separate sales/consumption reports, reliable delivery, refund policy, notifications and reconciliation. Freeze operation pricing and submit fixed credit quantities to Autumn to avoid mutable-rate settlement behavior.

With Lago Community

Delegate: usage aggregation and rating, plans, subscription billing, invoice generation, invoice-level wallets and payment integration.

Pagos additionally keeps: the hard-spend admission engine, reservation lifecycle and shared child-cap accounting, plus automation excluded by confirmed Premium gates.

06 / DECISION AND RELEASE GATES

Proceed with Autumn for Model C, subject to these gates

Fix the accounting boundary

Reservation release, expiry and partial settlement must respect the funding grant’s period. Test a hold across reset, upgrade, cancellation and grant expiry, including races and retries.

Prove the multi-product model

Retain the passing independent-plan, invoice-alignment and scoped-credit tests as regression coverage. Keep the approved immediate top-up and proration exceptions visible in the billing experience. Use staged test-clock advances so billing webhooks can run before invoice finalization.

Build one metering contract

Pagos owns begin / record / complete / cancel operations, a central append-only usage ledger, approved rate versions, stable operation/component IDs and an outbox. Add central free-grant eligibility, scope policy and separate sale/consumption reports. Freeze the price version per operation and reserve fixed credit amounts. Separate provider cost from customer charge. The validation adapter is not yet a production integration.

Qualify the deployment

Pin a release, package every required service, wire notifications, test queue/timer failures and coordinated restore, then measure reservation latency and sustained throughput against our real workload.

Shadow one product, then cut over

Compare the new ledger and charges with current behavior before making Autumn authoritative. Import balances and subscriptions with reconciliation, stop Memberstack collection for migrated subscriptions, and keep a controlled rollback path.

RATE-CHANGE INVESTIGATION · 21 OCTOBER 2026

Reservations do not guarantee an immutable price quote

Our API calls and units were valid. The documented override_value is the actual amount in the reserved feature's units. The unsupported assumption was that any later pricing or subscription change would preserve the original reservation's financial terms. No direct upstream fix was identified or applied.

The original flat-rate reproduction changed a customer's plan with billing.update(customize). The API preserves the remaining amount of 90, but replaces the underlying funding row. When the old row cannot receive its refund, the engine forwards the unused feature units to current funding, which prices them under current rates. Direct engine tests distinguish this from the stable-row path: flat-rate refunds against the original row already use the captured debit cost.

Native pricing alternative

Use Autumn's documented dimensions as immutable pricing versions, such as rate_version=v1 and v2. Publishing v2 must retain v1's name, rate, matching rules and relevant multipliers. Old holds keep v1; new work selects v2.

Pagos must choose and persist the approved version, reject unknown/missing versions, and drain holds before customer-specific grant replacement. This is a tested integration convention, not a built-in lock price-snapshot guarantee.

Controls that deliberately failed: removing the v1 dimension reprices the old partial refund (98). An unknown dimension value silently uses the configured default, as documented; it is not rejected by Autumn. Plan versioning grandfathers customers, but should not be confused with automatically pinning every open reservation.

REPLACEMENT + RESET FIX · 21 OCTOBER 2026

Open holds can survive the tested plan replacements

Implemented and validated locally: no hold drain required for the qualified recurring-grant transitions. A reservation stayed open while the local runtime was upgraded, its plan was replaced and its Stripe subscription renewed. New-period spending left 96 credits; settlement correctly left 96. The monthly-only baseline incorrectly returned 100.

This is an additional local patch on top of PR #4226 at 13c71d08. It records an explicit relationship between old and new funding rows. A usage-carrying replacement retains the original grant period; a fresh-allowance replacement records that the old funding is retired. Settlement follows that relationship and the actual refill marker, preserving valid refunds while discarding expired shares. It does not guess that two rows belong together because they fund the same feature.
